UW MATH AI Lab / PatternMiner

Can a model help us discover mathematics?

What if we looked at what a small model has learned, rather than treating it as a machine that only returns predictions?

The idea

From a question to a conjecture.

PatternMiner explores how language-model agents, specialized small models, and interpretability methods can work together in mathematical discovery. Instead of asking a model to produce an answer immediately, the system investigates a question through datasets, training, pattern extraction, and repeated tests.

The work is especially exciting to me because it treats a model as something we can investigate, rather than a black box whose outputs we simply accept.

The workflow

Propose. Test. Revise.

01 · Explore

Generate mathematical examples and train small models around a question.

02 · Interpret

Use patterns found in the trained models to formulate candidate rules.

03 · Challenge

Search for counterexamples, refine conjectures, and investigate possible proofs.

Why it matters to me

A prediction is a beginning, not an explanation.

One question I find compelling is whether understanding a trained model can lead us to a better mathematical rule than studying its predictions alone. Working on this project has pushed me to think about how we evaluate proposed explanations, test them on unseen cases, and recognize when an appealing pattern fails.

The paper studies the system on problems in algebraic combinatorics, including positivity questions. It also discusses how conjectures emerge and where the discovery process can fail.
