Curiosity / Patterns & Possibilities

Mathematics.

Why math? Well, math is interesting. Really.

What I love about mathematics is that it gives structure to complicated questions. I enjoy the process of working through a problem, trying different approaches, and understanding why something works, not just arriving at the answer.

Mathematics has also changed the way I approach questions outside the classroom. It helps me decide what to measure, make my assumptions explicit, and think critically about whether a model or a method is actually doing what I hoped it would do.

And I especially love seeing those ideas come to life. Sometimes that means studying how AI models learn mathematics. Other times, it means asking whether we can build a better soccer team.

UW PRIME / Soccer Analytics

What if the best eleven players don't make the best team?

I've watched soccer for as long as I can remember. Whenever I imagined my dream team, I usually started with players I already loved, then picked the strongest lineup from that group. But I had never really thought about what it would mean to build a team from scratch.

If I could choose any eleven players in the world, should I simply pick the highest-rated player at every position? That sounds reasonable, but would eleven individually brilliant players necessarily make the best team?

Soccer is a team sport, after all. Different playing styles, strengths, and roles need to fit together. That curiosity became the starting point for my PRIME project, where I explored how mathematics could help us think about player similarity, position fit, and building a balanced lineup.

01 / Represent

How do we describe a player with numbers?

I started with player attributes such as pace, shooting, passing, dribbling, defending, and physicality. These are not a complete portrait of a player, but they provide a shared starting point for comparison.

Begin with the player dataset and the distribution of positions. Every later choice depends on whom the data represent.

02 / Compare

Which players are actually similar?

Two players can have nearly identical overall ratings, yet very different strengths. I explored principal component analysis to visualize similarities among combinations of player attributes instead of relying on a single number.

03 / Understand roles

Not every great player plays the same role.

I explored position fingerprints, attacking and defensive profiles, and goalkeeper styles. Different roles emphasize different skills, so team building means more than comparing overall player ratings.

04 / Optimize

So, who belongs on the pitch together?

Finally, I explored how to build a lineup that accounts for player quality, positions, and complementary strengths. Comparing a selected lineup with independently chosen position specialists makes the team-level tradeoffs visible.
