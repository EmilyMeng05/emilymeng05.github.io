Curiosity / People & behavior

Psychology.

Why psych? Well, we're people. Our emotions, thoughts, behaviors, and past relationships all shape how we experience the world. To me, studying psychology is also a way of studying ourselves and understanding others.

I was first drawn to psychology because I wanted to understand my own emotions and reactions. Over time, that curiosity grew into an interest in how other people think, how we relate to one another, and how our experiences shape the society we share.

I'm especially interested in neuroscience and the connection between behavior and the brain. Studying animal behavior this quarter has made me curious about what we can learn from other species. How do animals learn, make decisions, and respond to their environments? What might those observations reveal about human behavior, and where do the comparisons stop being useful?

GRaND Lab / Meta-analysis

When studies ask similar questions, can we compare their answers?

Polygenic risk scores and adolescent psychopathology

In the GRaND Lab, I work on a meta-analysis exploring the relationship between polygenic risk scores (PRS) and psychopathology in adolescence. I have contributed to both literature screening and data extraction. I reviewed research papers against our inclusion criteria and extracted study-level information to help organize the evidence for analysis.

Working on this project has made me think more carefully about what it means to combine findings from different studies. I've learned that research is not just about choosing a statistical method. It also requires understanding where the data come from, what researchers actually measured, and what conclusions the evidence can support.
