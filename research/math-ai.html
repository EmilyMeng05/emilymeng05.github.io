UW MATH AI Lab / Small Mathematical Worlds

How do models learn mathematical structure?

If a model learns many related mathematical tasks, does it learn richer representations—or simply get better at recognizing familiar patterns?

The question

One world, many tasks.

Much of the work on understanding neural networks uses a single mathematical task. But models in the real world encounter many related problems. We wanted a more controlled way to ask what happens when task variety increases.

We built two small mathematical task ecosystems: IntWorld, based on integers, and PermWorld, based on permutations. They contain distinct but related tasks. Because the datasets are generated procedurally, we can change the number and kinds of tasks while controlling the learning environment.

How we investigated it

From controlled experiments to representations.

01 · Build a world

Create related mathematical tasks in a shared environment.

02 · Vary the learning

Control task diversity during model training.

03 · Look beyond accuracy

Examine internal representations and performance on related tasks.

What interested me

Learning is more than memorizing an answer.

I enjoy working at the boundary between building models and asking what their performance actually means. This project gave me a way to investigate learning using mathematics as a controlled environment, where the structure of the tasks is known rather than hidden.

Our initial results support the idea that increasing task diversity can enrich the representations learned by a model, while also raising more questions about how far that learning generalizes.
