Curiosity / Learning & Discovery

Computer Science.

Why computer science? Because an idea becomes a little more interesting when I can actually build it and see what happens.

I have always enjoyed asking how things work. Computer science gives me a way to turn those questions into experiments: writing a program, designing a model, testing an assumption, and then discovering what I overlooked.

My work in machine learning has made me especially curious about the difference between getting the right answer and actually understanding a problem. How does a model learn a mathematical pattern? And can looking inside what it learns help us discover something new?

Two questions / Two projects

Both projects grew out of the UW MATH AI Lab. They explore different sides of the same curiosity: what can models learn from mathematics, and what can mathematics learn from models?

01 / Learning

How do models learn mathematical structure?

In Small Mathematical Worlds, we study how learning related integer and permutation tasks changes a model's representations and its ability to generalize.

My main contribution focused on IntWorld, where I developed integer-based mathematical tasks, conducted trial runs, and wrote the IntWorld section of our paper. Through this work, I explored how task diversity affects what models learn and how well they transfer that knowledge to unfamiliar problems.

02 / Discovery

Can a model help us discover mathematics?

PatternMiner combines an LLM agent with smaller models and interpretability tools to propose mathematical conjectures. The system searches for counterexamples and uses what it finds to revise its ideas. My contribution focused on designing and conducting experiments involving Kronecker coefficients, investigating how this framework could support mathematical discovery.
